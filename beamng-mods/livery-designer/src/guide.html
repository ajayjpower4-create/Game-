The quick way (3 steps)

  1. Drag the vehicle's .zip file into the app. The app finds the vehicle's template, its paint and its Paint Design slot by itself.
  2. Design your livery. Mark the two sides, then add text, stripes, shapes and logos. Everything stays editable.
  3. Click "Make mod file" and drag the new file from your Downloads folder into your BeamNG mods folder.

Then in the game: Vehicle Config (Ctrl+W) > Parts > Paint Design > your livery.

What you need

  • BeamNG.drive
  • This app (LiveryDesigner.html). Double-click it and it opens in Chrome, Edge or Firefox. It runs on your computer and does not need the internet.

Step 1: Find the vehicle's .zip file

Modded vehicles

They are in your BeamNG mods folder. To open it, start the BeamNG.drive launcher, click Manage User Folder, then Open in Explorer, then open mods. (It is usually C:\Users\YOU\AppData\Local\BeamNG.drive\VERSION\mods or, on older versions, Documents\BeamNG.drive\mods.) Vehicles you downloaded from the in-game repository are in mods\repo.

Stock vehicles (the game's own)

They are in Steam\steamapps\common\BeamNG.drive\content\vehicles\. Each zip is named after the vehicle's folder, for example pickup.zip. The app only reads these files; it never changes them.

Tip: Not sure which zip is the vehicle? In the game, open the console with the ~ key and type print(be:getPlayerVehicle(0):getJBeamFilename()). It prints the folder name of the vehicle you are driving. Stock vehicles use that name for their zip.

Step 2: Drag it into the app

Drag the vehicle's .zip anywhere onto the app window, or click Load vehicle file… at the top. Don't unzip it first. The app reads only the parts it needs, so even big vehicle files load quickly.

The app then:

  • reads the vehicle's name and folder name,
  • finds its paint material (the one its own skins use, or the main body material),
  • picks the best template picture: a UV template if the vehicle has one, otherwise its body or skin texture,
  • reads the slot type its skins use, so your livery shows up in the right menu.

Want a different template picture? In the Sheet tab, pick another one from From vehicle. If the zip has more than one vehicle in it, the app asks which one you want.

You can also drag in other files: a .livery.json project, a picture to use as a logo, or a template picture.

Trucks and big mods: Some vehicles use more than one texture (for example a cab, a box and a trailer). In the Sheet tab, click Add sheet, then pick that part's template under From vehicle and tick its material.

Step 3: Mark the two sides

  1. Click Set side areas above the canvas.
  2. Drag the blue LEFT box over the vehicle's left side on the template and the orange RIGHT box over the right side. Drag the corners to resize them.
  3. Now use the Left side, Right side or Both sides view to work on each side.
  • Mirror to the other side (M) copies the selected item to the same spot on the other side. The two copies stay linked: change the text, font, colors or size on one, and the other side updates too. Untick Linked in the item's properties to edit the sides separately.
  • If the right side on your template points the same way as the left (the front of the vehicle is on the same end of the picture), set Right side layout to Same direction in the Sheet tab.
  • If mirrored text shows up backwards on one side in the game, tick Flip graphics on the other side in the Sheet tab.

Editing text

Select the text and type in the Text box. Double-clicking text on the canvas jumps straight to that box. You can change the font, size, bold, italic, letter spacing, line spacing, slant, alignment, fill (solid, gradient, checkered or stripes), outline, a second outer outline and a drop shadow. Press Enter in the Text box for more lines.

Fonts: Pick a font from the list, choose Other font… to type the name of any font installed on your computer, or click Load font file… to use a .ttf/.otf file. Loaded fonts are saved with your livery.

Layers

The top of the list is drawn on top. Drag rows to change the order, or use Up/Down. The eye hides a layer. The lock stops it from being clicked on the canvas. L/R show which side a mirrored item is on, and the chain means it is linked.

Base color

In the Sheet tab, pick the base color of the whole vehicle. Or tick Use the in-game paint color for empty areas: the empty parts of your livery then show whatever paint you choose in the game.

Step 5: Make the mod file

Click Make mod file at the top, give your livery a name in the Export tab, and pick one of these:

  • Put the livery inside the vehicle's file (the default for modded vehicles). You get a new copy of the vehicle's own zip, with the same name and your livery built in. Everything else in it stays exactly the same.
  • Separate livery file (the default for stock vehicles and vehicles from the repository). A small extra zip that adds the livery to the vehicle. It works for every vehicle.

Click the big Make mod file button. The file goes to your Downloads folder, and the app shows you what to do next.

Step 6: Put it in your mods folder

  1. Close BeamNG.drive.
  2. Open your mods folder (launcher > Manage User Folder > Open in Explorer > mods).
  3. Drag the new file from Downloads into the mods folder. Don't unzip it.
    • Livery inside the vehicle's file: Windows asks to replace the old file. Click Replace the file. If your browser renamed the download (for example truck (1).zip), rename it back first, so there is only one copy of the vehicle.
    • Separate livery file: it sits next to the vehicle. Nothing gets replaced.
  4. Start the game.
Never put anything into the game's own content\vehicles folder, and don't put a copy of a stock vehicle's zip in your mods folder. That's why the app makes a separate livery file for stock vehicles.

Step 7: Use it in the game

  1. Spawn the vehicle.
  2. Open Vehicle Config (Ctrl+W, or from the menu) and go to Parts.
  3. Find Paint Design (some mods call it Skin or Livery) and pick your livery's name.
  4. By default the livery looks exactly like it does in the app. If you ticked Use the in-game paint color, pick a paint in the Paint menu and it shows in the empty areas.

Changing a livery later

Drag the file you made (the vehicle's zip with the livery inside, or the separate livery zip) back into the app. It asks if you want to keep editing, and your whole design comes back: every layer, every text, every picture. Make your changes, click Make mod file again and replace the old file in your mods folder.

If you edit a separate livery file, also drag in the vehicle's own zip so the template shows again. You can save a project file too (Save project at the top).

If the game still shows the old version, clear the cache: launcher > Manage User Folder > Clear Cache.

Doing it by hand (without the vehicle's zip)

If you only have the vehicle's files unpacked, you can load them one at a time:

  1. Sheet tab > Load template…: the UV template or body texture (PNG, JPG or DDS).
  2. Sheet tab > Load vehicle materials file…: the vehicle's main.materials.json.
  3. Export tab > Vehicle details: type the vehicle folder name (the folder inside vehicles\). Optionally click Read slot type from a skin .jbeam….
  4. Make a Separate livery file and put it in your mods folder. For an unpacked mod you can instead copy the livery's folder (vehicles\VEHICLE\SKINID\) into mods\unpacked\MOD\vehicles\VEHICLE\.

Troubleshooting

  • "There is no vehicle in this file." That zip has no vehicles\NAME\ folder inside. Some mods are packed wrong, with an extra folder at the top. BeamNG can't load those either.
  • The livery isn't in the Paint Design list. Restart the game after adding the file. Make sure the vehicle itself is installed and turned on in the Mods menu. If you made a separate livery file, its vehicle folder must match the vehicle.
  • The app says the vehicle has no Paint Design slot. That mod was not made for skins. Advanced fix: open the vehicle's main .jbeam (the part with "slotType": "main") and add ["paint_design", "", "Paint Design"] to its "slots" list. If the part uses "slots2" instead, copy how another slot in that list is written.
  • The livery is on the wrong parts, or looks scrambled. The template was not the UV layout of that material. Pick another picture under From vehicle in the Sheet tab, or tick a different material.
  • The vehicle turns white, black or orange-checkered. The game can't find the livery picture. Make the mod file again and don't rename or unzip anything inside it.
  • The vehicle shows up twice, or behaves oddly. There are two copies of the vehicle's zip in your mods folder. Delete the old one.
  • Text is backwards on one side. Tick Flip graphics on the other side in the Sheet tab, or select that copy and click Flip H.
  • The zip won't open ("packed in a way this app can't read"). The vehicle was zipped with an unusual compression. Unzip it, zip it again with Windows (right-click > Send to > Compressed folder) or 7-Zip using normal ZIP / Deflate, and drag that in.
  • A DDS template won't open (BC7). Pick another picture under From vehicle, or open the DDS in paint.net or GIMP, save it as PNG and load the PNG.
  • Lost your work? The app saves your design in the browser as you go and offers to bring it back the next time you open it.
