What you need

  • BeamNG.drive
  • This app (LiveryDesigner.html). Double-click it and it opens in Chrome, Edge or Firefox. It runs on your computer and does not need the internet.
  • 7-Zip (free from 7-zip.org) to open and add files to vehicle zip files. Windows File Explorer also works.

Step 1: Find the vehicle's files

Every vehicle is a .zip file. Inside it is a folder called vehicles\NAME\. That NAME is the vehicle folder name. Write it down because you will type it in the Export tab.

Modded vehicles

They are in your BeamNG mods folder. To open it, start the BeamNG.drive launcher, click Manage User Folder, then Open in Explorer, then open mods. (It is usually C:\Users\YOU\AppData\Local\BeamNG.drive\VERSION\mods or, on older versions, Documents\BeamNG.drive\mods.)

Stock vehicles

They are in Steam\steamapps\common\BeamNG.drive\content\vehicles\. Each zip is named after its folder, so pickup.zip has the folder pickup. Only copy files out of these. Never change them.

Tip: In the game, open the console with the ~ key and type print(be:getPlayerVehicle(0):getJBeamFilename()). It prints the folder name of the vehicle you are driving.

Copy these files out to your desktop

Right-click the vehicle's zip, choose 7-Zip > Open archive, and go into vehicles\NAME\.

  1. The UV template. This picture shows where every body panel sits on the texture. Look for a file with skin_UVs, uv, template or skin in its name. If there is none, use the vehicle's main body texture. It usually ends in _b.color.png, _d.dds, _c.dds or has main in the name. A texture from one of the vehicle's existing skin folders works too.
  2. The materials file, main.materials.json (or any file ending in .materials.json). It tells the app the vehicle's real body material, so your livery keeps the shine, panel lines and details.
  3. (Optional) An existing skin's .jbeam file. If the vehicle already has skins, open one of the skin subfolders and copy its .jbeam. The app reads the correct slot type from it.

Step 2: Set up the app

  1. Open LiveryDesigner.html.
  2. Go to the Sheet tab and click Load template…. Pick the UV template (PNG, JPG or DDS). The sheet is set to the template's size.
  3. Still in the Sheet tab, click Load vehicle materials file… and pick the .materials.json. The app ticks the paintable body material for you (marked paint).
  4. Choose how the template is shown:
    • Guide on top: see-through lines while you work. They are not saved into your livery.
    • Background: the template picture becomes part of the livery. Use this when you loaded the real body texture and want to paint on top of it.
Trucks and big mods: Some vehicles use more than one texture (for example a cab, a box and a trailer). Click Add sheet in the Sheet tab and give each texture its own sheet, its own template and its own material.

Step 3: Mark the two sides

  1. Click Set side areas above the canvas.
  2. Drag the blue LEFT box over the vehicle's left side on the template and the orange RIGHT box over the right side. Drag the corners to resize them.
  3. Now use the Left side, Right side or Both sides view to work on each side.
  • Mirror to the other side (M) copies the selected item to the same spot on the other side. The two copies stay linked: change the text, font, colors or size on one, and the other side updates too. Untick Linked in the item's properties to edit the sides separately.
  • If the right side on your template points the same way as the left (the front of the vehicle is on the same end of the picture), set Right side layout to Same direction in the Sheet tab.
  • If mirrored text shows up backwards on one side in the game, tick Flip graphics on the other side in the Sheet tab.

Editing text

Select the text and type in the Text box. Double-clicking text on the canvas jumps straight to that box. You can change the font, size, bold, italic, letter spacing, line spacing, slant, alignment, fill (solid, gradient, checkered or stripes), outline, a second outer outline and a drop shadow. Press Enter in the Text box for more lines.

Fonts: Pick a font from the list, choose Other font… to type the name of any font installed on your computer, or click Load font file… to use a .ttf/.otf file. Loaded fonts are saved inside your project.

Layers

The top of the list is drawn on top. Drag rows to change the order, or use Up/Down. The eye hides a layer. The lock stops it from being clicked on the canvas. L/R show which side a mirrored item is on, and the chain means it is linked.

Base color

In the Sheet tab, pick the base color of the whole vehicle. Or tick Use the in-game paint color for empty areas: the empty parts of your livery then show whatever paint you choose in the game.

Step 5: Export

Open the Export tab and fill in:

  • Vehicle folder: exactly like the folder inside the zip, for example pickup.
  • Livery name: the name you will see in the game's parts menu.
  • Skin ID: made for you from the name (lowercase, no spaces). Every livery for the same vehicle needs a different Skin ID.
  • Slot type: leave it as paint_design unless the vehicle uses something else. Click Read slot type from a skin .jbeam… to check.

Click Download livery ZIP. You get a zip like this:

vehicles/VEHICLE/SKINID/
    VEHICLE_skin_SKINID.jbeam        adds the livery to the Paint Design menu
    skin.materials.json              tells the game to use your picture
    VEHICLE_skin_SKINID.color.png    your livery picture

Also click Save project at the top so you can come back and edit it later. The project file keeps every layer, text and picture.

Step 6: Install it (paste it inside the vehicle file)

A. Modded vehicle: paste it inside the vehicle's mod file

  1. Close BeamNG.drive.
  2. In your mods folder, copy the vehicle's zip somewhere safe as a backup.
  3. Right-click the vehicle's zip and choose 7-Zip > Open archive. Double-click into vehicles, then into the vehicle folder.
  4. Open your livery zip in a second 7-Zip window and go into vehicles, then the vehicle folder.
  5. Drag the SKINID folder from the livery zip into the vehicle zip window. Click Yes/OK when 7-Zip asks to copy.
  6. Close 7-Zip and start the game.

Windows File Explorer can do the same: double-click a zip to open it like a folder, then drag the SKINID folder in.
If the vehicle mod is unpacked (a normal folder in mods\unpacked\), paste the SKINID folder into mods\unpacked\MOD\vehicles\VEHICLE\.

B. Stock vehicle (or if you would rather not touch the mod file)

Put the livery zip itself into your mods folder. Do not unzip it. BeamNG adds it to the vehicle by itself. This works for stock and modded vehicles.

Do not paste into the game's own content\vehicles\*.zip files. Steam puts the original files back on every update or file check, so your livery would disappear, and a broken game file can stop the vehicle from loading.

Step 7: Use it in the game

  1. Spawn the vehicle.
  2. Open Vehicle Config (Ctrl+W, or from the menu) and go to Parts.
  3. Find Paint Design (some mods call it Skin or Livery) and pick your livery's name.
  4. By default the livery looks exactly like it does in the app. If you ticked Use the in-game paint color, pick a paint in the Paint menu and it shows in the empty areas.

Changing a livery later

Open your saved project, make your changes and export again with the same Skin ID. Replace the old SKINID folder inside the vehicle zip (method A), or overwrite the old livery zip in mods (method B). If the game still shows the old version, clear the cache: launcher > Manage User Folder > Clear Cache.

Troubleshooting

  • The livery isn't in the Paint Design list. Check that the vehicle folder name is spelled exactly right, that the SKINID folder sits inside vehicles\VEHICLE\, and that you restarted the game. Some mods use a different slot type: load one of their skin .jbeam files in the Export tab.
  • The vehicle has no Paint Design slot at all. That mod was not made for skins. Advanced fix: open the vehicle's main .jbeam (the part with "slotType": "main") and add ["paint_design", "", "Paint Design"] to its "slots" list. If the part uses "slots2" instead, copy how another slot in that list is written.
  • The livery is on the wrong parts, or looks scrambled. The template was not the UV layout of that material. Use a texture from the same material, or tick a different material in the Sheet tab.
  • The vehicle turns white, black or orange-checkered. The game can't find the picture. Don't rename the exported files or the SKINID folder, and keep them together.
  • Text is backwards on one side. Tick Flip graphics on the other side in the Sheet tab, or select that copy and click Flip H.
  • The paint looks too dull or too shiny. Load the vehicle's materials file (Sheet tab) so the app copies the vehicle's real body material instead of a generic one.
  • A DDS file won't open (BC7). Open it in paint.net or GIMP, save it as PNG, and load the PNG.
  • Lost your work? The app saves your design in the browser as you go and offers to bring it back the next time you open it. To be safe, use Save project too.
